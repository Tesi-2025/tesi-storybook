# Chapter 7 — Donkey Shoes

It was, of course, Draghetta who asked. She had woken up around six with a small smoke-hiccup, refilled the coffee, poked Topo awake (Topo protested operatically), and settled herself on the floor beside Mamma's chair with a notebook.

"Mamma," she said. "Explain it to me one more time. Like I am on the YC review panel. What is Donkey Shoes, exactly?"

Mamma put down her stylus. She turned in her chair so she was facing all of them — Babbo at the moka, Draghetta on the floor, Topo now on the counter with his notebook, and Tesi on her shoulder in the seat of power.

"*Allora.*"

She took a breath. It was, if you looked closely, the same kind of breath Topo had taken before the aria. Some breaths are for singing and some breaths are for pitching, and there is not, if you think about it, very much difference between the two.

"Animals wear shoes," Mamma said. "Not just horses. Dogs wear shoes in cities where the pavement gets hot. Cats wear shoes after surgery. Working dogs on farms wear shoes. Elephants in sanctuaries wear shoes. Giraffes —" she gestured at her own hooves, in their soft Donkey Shoes with the reinforced heel — "wear shoes because for many years we have had *no dignified footwear at all* and it has been, quite frankly, *un disastro.*"

"*Vero,*" said Draghetta, writing this down.

"But every animal shoe on the market is designed by humans, for humans' idea of what an animal foot should be. The dog shoes fall off. The horse shoes are essentially medieval. The giraffe shoes — there are three companies making giraffe shoes worldwide, all of them terrible, and I have tried all of them and my ankles will never forgive me."

"*Amen,*" said Babbo, from the moka.

"Donkey Shoes is different. We use pressure mapping — Babbo built the sensors — and we 3D-print the sole for the individual animal. Every shoe fits its animal. Every shoe is comfortable. Every shoe lasts. And we started with donkeys, because donkeys are — *piccoli angeli*, working animals, tremendously overlooked. Every profit from every donkey shoe goes back into the sanctuary that fitted the donkey. That is the *anima* of the company. Then we scale to horses, dogs, working animals, and eventually — *eventually* — we do a small, expensive, beautiful line for giraffes." She wiggled a hoof. "Which is what I am wearing now. And they are *magnifiche.*"

"So it's not really about donkeys," said Draghetta.

"It is *all* about donkeys," said Mamma. "The donkey is the *anima*. The donkey is the *why.* The rest is the *how.*"

Tesi, on her shoulder, made his executive chirp. He had been at every founding meeting. He knew this pitch by heart. He had, on one occasion, bitten a potential angel investor who had suggested Mamma should "consider a bigger market" — the angel had laughed, invested anyway, and now had a small parrotlet-shaped scar on his thumb which he showed to people at parties as a badge of honour.

"That is the pitch," said Mamma. "Two sentences: *comfortable footwear for animals of all species, starting with the ones we owe the most to.* Everything else is footnotes."

Draghetta wrote it down. She underlined the important bits. She did not exhale on the notebook.

"Mamma," she said. "That's *bellissimo.*"

"*Grazie, amore.*"

Tesi bit Mamma on the ear again. It was a good pitch. She had earned it.
