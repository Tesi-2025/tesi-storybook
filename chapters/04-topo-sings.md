# Chapter 4 — Topo Sings

Four hours of work do not sound like much. Four hours of work by a giraffe who codes at eighty words per minute with hooves, however, is a *great deal* of work. When Mamma finally opened the recovered files and saw all the small careful edits that were now gone — the paragraph she had rewritten seven times about why animals need better footwear, the traction chart with the beautiful hockey-stick, the sentence about Babbo's espresso — she put her head down on the table and made a sound that giraffes are not supposed to be able to make.

It was Topo who saved them.

He climbed up onto the fruit bowl (which was still slightly damaged from his earlier trajectory), he took a deep breath — for a mouse, this is more of a squeak-shaped inhalation — and he began to sing.

He sang *Che gelida manina* from *La Bohème*. He sang it in the little upper falsetto that only mice and very small children can achieve, and he sang it *correctly*, all the phrasing exactly right, all the tender pauses, all the Puccini heart. His whiskers vibrated with each note. His tiny paws were pressed over his tiny heart.

Draghetta stopped smoking. Babbo, in the doorway with the moka, went very still. Tesi, from the top of the fridge, cocked his head at exactly ninety degrees, which is the parrotlet equivalent of standing to attention.

Mamma lifted her long neck slowly, the way a person waking up from a bad dream lifts their head, and she listened.

Topo held the high note.

He held it longer than was strictly necessary. He held it because sometimes an aria is not about the notes at all, it is about the small mouse in the fruit bowl who wants his family to remember that they love each other and that everything can be rebuilt.

When he finished, the kitchen was silent.

"*Topo,*" said Mamma, softly. "*Amore mio. Grazie.*"

Topo bowed. He did the full bow, the one with the paw across the belly and the whiskers all the way forward. Then he squeaked something that in mouse-Italian meant *anytime, sister, anytime.*

Tesi flew down from the fridge and landed on Topo's head, which was small enough that Tesi almost eclipsed him entirely. He gave one small chirp of approval — the specific chirp reserved for good performances — and Topo squeaked back, and for a moment the two brothers just sat there being small together on the fruit bowl.

Babbo poured a fresh round of espresso. He poured one for Mamma, one for Draghetta, a thimble for Topo, and — because Babbo believes that every family member should be included even if physiologically they should not have caffeine — the smallest possible drop, on a teaspoon, for Tesi.

Tesi did not drink it. But he was moved by the gesture.

"*Bene,*" said Mamma, straightening. "*Rifacciamo tutto. Da capo. Andiamo.*"

From the top, indeed. Like an aria.
