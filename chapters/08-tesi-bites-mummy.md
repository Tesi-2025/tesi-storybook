# Chapter 8 — Tesi Bites Mummy

By noon on the second day, the application was almost, almost, almost done.

The pitch was clean. The deck was tight. The demo notebook had been rebuilt from Draghetta's dictation ("no, sister, the y-axis was *logarithmic*, don't argue with me, I remember"). The founder video had been shot in one take on Babbo's phone, propped up against a stack of books, with Mamma explaining Donkey Shoes calmly and without a single filler word — and with Tesi, unrehearsed, waddling into frame at exactly the right moment to peer at the camera and chirp once. Babbo had watched the playback and said, quietly, "*Amore. That is going to close it.*"

Only one thing remained: the ten-line summary.

Ten lines. That was all. Ten lines to describe the entire company, the entire market, the entire team, the entire vision. Y-Combinator asks for ten lines because ten lines is where founders reveal whether they actually know what they are doing.

Mamma had been staring at the box for forty minutes.

She had typed a sentence. She had deleted it. She had typed another. She had deleted that too. She had, at one point, put her forehead directly on the keyboard, generating a line of text that read `jjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjjj`, and she had deleted that as well but with a sigh so long that Topo — asleep in the biscuit tin — squeaked in sympathy.

Draghetta hovered. She wanted to help. She did not know how.

Babbo was in the shower, because sometimes a co-founder's best contribution is not being in the room.

Tesi, on the perch, watched.

He watched Mamma type. He watched Mamma delete. He watched Mamma type. He watched Mamma delete. He watched the small line between Mamma's eyebrows get deeper and deeper, until it was less a line and more a small giraffe canyon.

Then Tesi did the only thing left to do.

He launched himself off the perch. He flew, in the low steady flight of a small green rocket, directly across the kitchen. He landed on Mamma's shoulder. He walked up her neck to her ear. And he bit it.

Not gently, this time. Not the little affectionate love-bite. He bit her the way a small very determined bird bites a beloved giraffe who is about to disappear inside her own head — with commitment. With intent. With just enough parrotlet beak to be *unignorable.*

"*Ay!*" said Mamma. And she laughed.

She laughed the way she used to laugh before the deadline. She laughed the way she laughed at Babbo's espresso jokes. She laughed the deep giraffe laugh that comes from the very bottom of a very long neck, and the small canyon between her eyebrows smoothed out.

"*Tesi. Amore. Basta.*"

Tesi bit her again, for insurance.

"*Va bene, va bene, ho capito.*"

She sat back. She looked at the box. She looked at the screen with fresh eyes — the way you look at a merge conflict after a walk. She began to type. This time she did not delete.

Ten lines came out of her, one after the other, calm and clear. They were the ten lines she had known all along; she had just needed a small green interrupter to knock her out of the loop.

When she finished, she looked at Tesi. Tesi looked back.

"*Grazie, piccolo. Sei il mio git-reset.*"

Tesi bit her a third time, because he loved her, and because he was CIBS-certified, and because that is what parrotlets do.
