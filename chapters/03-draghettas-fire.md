# Chapter 3 — Draghetta's Fire

It was, everybody later agreed, not really Draghetta's fault.

She had been standing behind Mamma, peering over her shoulder at the pitch deck — slide seven, the one with the market-size chart — and she had been about to say something clever about TAM versus SAM. She had opened her mouth. She had inhaled to speak.

Draghetta is a dragon. When Draghetta inhales, she also, on occasion, exhales.

There was a very small *fwoomph*.

There was a slightly larger *pop*.

The laptop screen went white, then black, then white again, and then displayed, with the terrible calm of expensive electronics, a single line:

```
kernel panic — not syncing: file system corruption detected
```

Mamma stood up so quickly that Topo, who had been napping in her mane, was flung across the kitchen and landed in the fruit bowl. He emerged from between two apples with the dazed expression of a mouse who has been asleep during a small apocalypse.

"*MAMMA MIA,*" said Mamma.

Draghetta went a colour that on a green dragon means very, very pale. Smoke leaked from her nostrils in a small apologetic curl. "I didn't — I wasn't — I was only going to say the market was *piccolo* — I —"

"*Amore. Amore. Respira.*" Mamma put one enormous hoof on Draghetta's tiny shoulder. "Tell me exactly what was open."

"The deck," whispered Draghetta. "The demo notebook. The founder video. The — the pitch script. Everything. It was all open. I'm so sorry, Mamma, I —"

"Backup?" said Babbo, appearing in the doorway with an espresso in each hand, one for the crisis and one for the recovery.

"Committed as of last night," said Mamma. "But this morning's edits — the traction numbers — the — *aspetta.*"

She unplugged the laptop. She plugged it back in. She held the power button. She did the thing where you hold three keys at once. The screen showed, briefly, a hopeful apple. Then the panic came back.

Tesi, who had watched all of this from the curtain rod, flew down and landed on the laptop lid. He looked at it. He looked at Mamma. He looked at Draghetta, who was trying not to cry, which for a dragon means her tears are evaporating on the way down her cheeks and making tiny hissing sounds.

Tesi walked deliberately to Draghetta and bit her, gently, on the snout.

Draghetta laughed. She could not help it. A parrotlet bite from a bird who weighs less than a slice of bread is the kind of thing that is impossible to take seriously, even from within the worst of days. The tears stopped hissing.

"*Grazie, Tesi,*" she whispered.

"*Right,*" said Mamma, cracking her hooves again. "Time-Machine backup was at oh-four-hundred. We lose four hours. We rebuild. *Andiamo.*"
