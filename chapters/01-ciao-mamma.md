# Chapter 1 — Ciao. Mamma.

The perch was still warm when Tesi opened one eye.

Outside the window the London morning was that particular kind of grey which happens when the clouds have not yet decided whether to commit. Inside, on the long kitchen table, a laptop fan whirred, three empty espresso cups stood in a small circle like a druidic council, and a giraffe was hunched over a keyboard with the very deep concentration of somebody who has been awake since four.

Tesi ruffled. Tesi is thirty grams of green Pacific parrotlet and thirty grams of opinions. He shook out his feathers one wing at a time, blinked twice at the ceiling, and made the small chirrup he reserves specifically for mornings.

"Ciao. Mamma."

The giraffe did not look up. Her long neck was arched in a perfect coding curve, her hooves — she is very good at typing with hooves, this has been established — were moving at approximately eighty words per minute. On her feet, of course, were her Donkey Shoes, the soft ones with the reinforced heel.

"*Ciao, piccolo,*" Mamma said, still typing. "*Un momento, amore. Mamma sta risolvendo un bug.*"

Tesi tilted his head. He had learned every single line of code from her. He also knew, from experience, that "*un momento*" from a coder-giraffe could mean anywhere from four seconds to four hours. He waddled sideways along the perch, hopped onto her horn, hopped down onto her shoulder, and peered at the screen.

Python. A stack trace. Something about `KeyError: 'traction'`. Tesi understood immediately. He pecked, delicately, at the trackpad.

"*Bravo!*" said Mamma, watching the cursor jump into the correct line. "*Che carino, hai visto il bug?*"

Tesi had seen the bug. Tesi always saw the bug. He was CIBS-certified — Certified in Bug Spotting, a designation he had awarded himself but which the family respected without question because he had never, in his entire life of eight months, been wrong.

From the corner of the kitchen came a small operatic wail. Topo, Tesi's little brother the mouse, was warming up his voice. Somewhere upstairs there was a distant, unmistakable *whoosh* of something being lightly toasted — Draghetta, up early, debugging her own stuffed animal.

Mamma sighed, saved the file, and finally, finally, turned her enormous soft eyes on Tesi.

"*Buongiorno, amore mio. Come stai?*"

Tesi bit her ear, gently, because he loved her.
