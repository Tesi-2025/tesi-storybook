# Chapter 2 — The Deadline

Babbo announced the deadline the way Babbo announces everything: quietly, over the second espresso of the morning, in the tone of a man delivering weather.

"*Amore. La domanda YC scade tra quarantotto ore.*"

The kitchen went still. Even the fridge, which had been humming a low B-flat, paused.

"Forty-eight," repeated Mamma. Her neck went very straight. When Mamma's neck goes very straight it means she is doing arithmetic, and when a giraffe does arithmetic in her head you can watch the numbers travel all the way down. "Forty-eight hours. We have — *dai, aspetta* — the pitch video, the founder video, the traction numbers, the deck, the demo, the ten-line summary, and the answer to that question about what we would build if we had ten times the money."

"We would buy more espresso," said Babbo, refilling his cup.

"*Non aiuti,*" said Mamma, but she smiled. She always smiled when Babbo made jokes about espresso. It was one of the small rituals of their marriage.

Tesi, on Mamma's shoulder, made the small executive chirp he uses in meetings. It is a chirp that means: *I am here, I am listening, I will contribute at the appropriate moment.* He had used it in every stand-up since he was three months old.

Topo scampered up the table leg carrying a tiny scroll on which he had, overnight, hand-illustrated the Donkey Shoes logo in twelve variations. He unrolled it. It was, objectively, beautiful. Topo squeaked something in his tiny falsetto that sounded like Puccini's *Nessun dorma* but rearranged for mouse and marketing.

"*Bravissimo, Topo,*" said Mamma. "*Number nine. We use number nine.*"

From the hallway came a small polite cough that was actually a small polite jet of flame. Draghetta had arrived. She was carrying a notebook and wearing her serious face, which for a dragon is the face where the eyebrow-ridges come together in the middle and a tiny wisp of smoke escapes each nostril.

"Advisory board is convened," she announced. "I have brought the roadmap. I have also brought marshmallows in case morale drops."

"*Che bello,*" said Mamma. She looked around the kitchen — at her husband refilling the moka, at her mouse-son with his scroll, at her dragon-daughter with her marshmallows, and at Tesi, thirty grams of green certainty, gripping her shoulder with feet that were exactly the right amount of tight.

"*Allora. Ci siamo. Andiamo.*"

Forty-eight hours. One family. One giraffe. Donkey Shoes to Y-Combinator.

She cracked her knuckles, which for a giraffe is a *very* impressive sound.
