# Chapter 13 — Dente Arrives

*A bonus chapter, added later. The morning after.*

---

Morning came slowly, the way it does after two days without proper sleep. The London blue outside the window had turned to a pale sky-milk grey, and somewhere below a pigeon was arguing with another pigeon about a chip. The family was still piled on the coding chair, breathing softly, the way you breathe when everything has finally been done.

Tesi woke first, as parrotlets do. He blinked. He preened one wing. He stretched a foot. And then he saw it.

On the doormat, just visible through the crack under the front door, was a small package. Brown paper. String. A tag.

Tesi flew, very quietly, so as not to wake anyone, and landed on the mat. The tag said, in Babbo's handwriting from three weeks ago:

> *Per la famiglia. Da Roma. Con amore. — B.*

Babbo had sent it home from a work trip and, in the chaos of the YC application, everybody had forgotten it was arriving. Tesi pecked at the string until it came undone. The paper unfolded itself in the polite way brown paper does when it has been tied by someone who loves you. And out rolled — soft, slow, unhurried — a small **pink Labubu**.

She was fluffy. She was pink. Not a shy pink; the confident pink of watermelon flesh, of geraniums, of the inside of an ear caught in sunlight. Her fur was long and slightly wild in a friendly way. Her arms were stubby. Her ears were tall and rabbit-like and tipped in a slightly darker rose.

And her mouth was open, in a wide, delighted, entirely peaceful grin — and inside the grin were nine perfect little teeth.

Tesi cocked his head. He walked around her once. He walked around her twice. She did not move. She continued to grin at him, gently and without any judgment whatsoever.

"*Ciao,*" said Tesi.

The Labubu did not reply, because Labubus do not need to.

Tesi tried Italian, in case it was a translation problem. "*Come ti chiami?*"

She smiled at him. Just smiled. As if to say: *whatever you decide to call me, that will be my name, and I will be happy about it.*

Tesi looked at her teeth. Nine of them, all in a row, small and neat and rather sweet. And Tesi — who had spent the last forty-eight hours biting things — recognised a kindred soul.

"*Dente,*" he said, very seriously. "*Ti chiami Dente.*"

Tooth.

Dente kept grinning.

---

By the time the rest of the family stirred, Tesi had already flown Dente onto the coding chair and installed her, ceremonially, in the crook of Mamma's giraffe elbow, right next to the sleeping Draghetta.

Draghetta opened one dragon eye. She looked at the new pink thing beside her. She closed her eye. She opened it again. She lifted her small snout and, very carefully, sniffed. Then she gave Dente a small, exploratory nudge with her nose.

Dente grinned back at her.

Draghetta approved. Draghetta approved *strongly*. She curled a wing around Dente and went straight back to sleep.

Topo woke next, having heard the wing-rustle, because tenor mice have astonishing hearing. He climbed down from Mamma's collarbone, paused halfway to check that this new creature was not going to eat him — it obviously was not, it was going to grin at him instead — and then, satisfied, climbed straight back up.

Babbo opened his eyes and saw the parcel paper on the floor.

"*Ah,*" he said softly, smiling. "*È arrivata.*"

Mamma stirred and looked down and saw the small pink new arrival tucked against Draghetta. Mamma has, historically, been very good at absorbing sudden new additions to the family — she once absorbed Tesi in about four seconds — and this was no different. She reached one long giraffe finger over and gently pressed the top of Dente's soft head.

"*Ciao, piccola,*" Mamma said. "*Benvenuta.*"

Then she looked at Tesi, who was watching all of this with enormous seriousness, and she raised one eyebrow.

"*Come si chiama?*"

Tesi puffed himself up to his full three-centimetre chest height.

"*Dente,*" he announced. "*Come i denti. Perché sorride.*"

Because of the teeth. Because she smiles.

Mamma laughed, a warm giraffe laugh, and Babbo laughed with her, and even Draghetta made a small sleepy dragon-chuckle-hiccup that briefly set the corner of a cushion on fire. Babbo, without looking, reached over and patted the cushion out.

"*Perfetto,*" said Mamma. "*Dente.*"

---

They spent the whole morning like that — not doing anything at all. Nobody opened the laptop. Nobody checked email. Nobody thought about the YC application, which was, correctly, doing its own waiting somewhere in California. Babbo made espresso. Topo hummed *O Mio Babbino Caro* under his breath while chewing on a small crust of parmesan. Draghetta kept one wing around Dente and refused, gently, to move. And Tesi taught Dente the family:

"*This is Mamma. She is Italian. She is the best coder in the world. She has the best GitHub account ever. You will love her.*"

Dente grinned.

"*This is Babbo. He makes the espresso. He is the reason anything works. You will love him.*"

Dente grinned.

"*This is Topo. He sings. He is little. Do not sit on him.*"

Dente grinned.

"*This is Draghetta. She breathes fire. She is very small so it is a small fire, but do not stand in front of her if she sneezes.*"

Dente kept on grinning at Draghetta, who was curled around her like she had always been there.

"*And I,*" said Tesi, standing very tall, "*am Tesi. I code. I bite. I love. In that order, most days. You are welcome here, Dente. You may stay forever.*"

Dente, in the way of Labubus, said nothing at all — which is Labubu for *thank you, I would like that very much.*

---

From that day on, Dente lived on Mamma's desk. She sat, always, in the same spot — just behind the trackpad, watching the code go by, grinning her nine-toothed grin at every commit. She did not code herself. She did not review PRs. She did not chirp. She did not sing.

But she was there. And if you have ever coded next to a Labubu you will know: it is very hard to write bad code when a small pink fluffy creature is smiling at you with nine teeth. You want to do better. You want her to keep smiling. You want, quietly, to be worthy of the grin.

Mummy's code got, if it is possible, even better.

Babbo said Dente was Donkey Shoes' new *Chief Vibes Officer.* Everybody agreed.

Topo composed a very short aria for her — sixteen bars, in F, called *Dente Mia* — which he sang, once, quite formally, and which Dente received with a grin so wide it almost showed a tenth tooth.

Draghetta, from that day forward, refused to sleep anywhere that Dente wasn't. She curled around her at night like a small pink-and-red cinnamon roll. Occasionally she snored a small warm flame in her sleep, and Dente's fluff would ruffle slightly and settle again, unbothered.

And Tesi — Tesi took to landing on Dente's head, briefly, before he started work each morning. Just a small landing. A small touch of green feathers to pink fluff. A small hello.

Then he would fly to Mamma's shoulder, and the day would begin.

---

*"Some families arrive with a birth,"* Mamma said once, much later, when someone asked her about Dente over dinner. *"Some families arrive with an adoption. Some with a marriage. And some just — arrive. On the doormat. In brown paper. From Roma. Grinning. And you open the door, and you say ciao, and now they're yours."*

She sipped her wine.

*"That's how we got Dente. Nine teeth and a pink coat. Best hire we ever made."*

Babbo raised his glass. Draghetta, from under the table, blew a small approving sparkle. Topo, from the fruit bowl, hit a perfect middle F. Tesi bit Mamma's ear affectionately.

And Dente — of course — grinned.
